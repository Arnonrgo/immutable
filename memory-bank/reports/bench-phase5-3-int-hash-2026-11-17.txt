Phase 5.3 (ints): keep the identity hash below 2^32, or mix it? Measurement and decision
=========================================================================================
toolchain: go1.27.4 darwin/arm64
date: 2026-11-17
load: average 220-260 on 10 cores (heavily loaded). Structure numbers (nodes, depth,
      bytes) are exact. Times are the minimum of 5 runs of 100 ms in one process,
      with the two hashers run one after the other, so ratios under about 1.5x on
      reads are not reliable: rand32 keys give the same trie shape under both
      hashers (mean depth 4.05) and still show Get at 64 vs 95 ns.

Question
  hashUint64 returns an int key below 2^32 unchanged and mixes larger keys with a
  SplitMix64 finalizer. The plan (5.3) proposed mixing every key, because keys that
  share low bits (multiples of 32, 1024, ...) pile into one slot per level, and
  said sequential keys were "about 24% slower today".

Method
  A throwaway test (deleted after the run) built maps of 1,000 and 100,000 int keys
  with two Hasher[int]s that differ only in whether values below 2^32 are mixed:
    identity = the current hashUint64     finalizer = SplitMix64 finalizer, always
  Key patterns: seq (0..N-1), ts (1.7e9 + i), x7919 (i*7919), x100, x32, x1024,
  x4096, rand32 (random below 2^31). Get and SetOne run on a 100,000-entry map in
  random order (SetOne inserts the next keys of the same pattern); Build adds the
  keys in pattern order, BuildShuffled in random order.

What the data says
  - Dense keys (seq, ts, x7919) are best under identity. At 1,000 keys it builds a
    trie of 33 nodes and 2 levels, the finalizer one of about 310 nodes and 2.65
    levels on average (up to 5). At 100,000 keys the shapes are the same size
    (depth 4.0 against 4.05).
  - Building in key order is 1.5-1.9x faster with identity for every pattern except
    rand32 (1.05x): neighbours in key order are neighbours in memory. Shuffled
    builds are level to 1.2x faster (x100 1.5x).
  - The finalizer wins only for keys that share low bits: x32 depth 5 -> 4.05,
    x1024 depth 6 -> 4.05, x4096 6 -> 4.05, and Get 1.4-1.6x faster on x32 and
    x1024 (noisy). It is slower to build on every pattern.
  - x100 and x4096 are more compact under identity (7.1 MB against 8.1 MB at
    100,000 keys), so low-bit sharing does not always cost memory.
  - The plan's premise (sequential keys slower than random) does not hold: in the
    baseline, sequential Get is 12.2 against 27.0 ns at 1,000 keys and a sequential
    Set loop 232 against 277 us.

Decision
  Keep the identity hash below 2^32 (5.3 ints declined). Dense integer keys are the
  common case, the finalizer makes them 1.2-1.9x slower to build and read, and
  would change the iteration order of every int-keyed map. The cost of keeping it
  is one or two more trie levels for keys with shared low bits, which the owner can
  avoid with a custom Hasher. The doc comment on hashUint64 records this and
  TestDefaultIntHashKeepsDenseKeysCompact (map_hash_test.go) pins the compact
  trie for dense keys; 3 of 3 mutants of hashUint64 (always mix, mix above 255,
  never mix) are killed by it.
